Back the mid-level categories with the database, not static code

Category tabs were code: a static list per root plus a hardcoded product →
subcategory map, so the 42 Daiso products (and everything imported since)
had no mid-level categories, and the tabs counted only the legacy samples —
including subcategories that belong to another root ("Hair Care" under
Makeup).

Categories are now the admin's tree: the Category model gains an image and
an active flag, and ProductSubcategory lets a product sit in any number of
mid-level categories while Product.categoryId stays its primary top-level
category (a cleansing tissue is both Cleansing and Masks & Patches).

- /admin/categories: create, rename, reorder, hide and delete roots and
  children, with the tile image the listing pages show; deleting a category
  that products still use is refused.
- The product form picks a top-level category, then its children (several
  allowed), and can add a child inline.
- The products list filters by subcategory, shows each product's chips and
  assigns one to many products at once; /admin/products/import maps a whole
  sheet (product slug, category, subcategory) with a dry-run preview that
  flags unknown slugs without dropping a product's other rows.
- The storefront reads the tree: header menu, listing tabs as image tiles,
  and only a category's own tabs — hidden when they have no products.
  Pseudo-categories (PRE-ORDER) derive their tabs from what their products
  carry; mid-level URLs redirect to the root with ?sub=.

scripts/migrate-subcategories.ts creates the children and moves the legacy
map (dry run first, then --apply); scripts/test-category-admin.ts exercises
the bulk assign and CSV paths end to end.
